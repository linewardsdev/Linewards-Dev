# UI Screenshot Review Rubric

Use this rubric when reviewing UI, HUD, game, or visual-art screenshots.

## Composition and layout

- Primary action is visually obvious.
- Important controls are not clipped, hidden, or crowded.
- Text and icons have enough spacing from edges and notches/safe areas.
- Overlays do not obscure gameplay-critical content.
- State changes are visible between before/after screenshots.

## Readability

- Text is legible at the target device size.
- Icons are recognizable without tooltips when they carry gameplay meaning.
- Contrast is sufficient in bright, dark, and busy areas.
- Labels do not fight with effects, particles, or moving units.

## Gameplay clarity

- The player can tell what happened, where it happened, and why it matters.
- Damage, death, leak, build, sell, send, income, and match-end cues are distinct.
- Reduced-effects mode preserves critical information.
- Repeated objects remain readable under stress or crowding.

## Visual consistency

- Palette, materials, shadows, and effects feel part of the same art direction.
- New assets match the surrounding UI/game language.
- Decorative detail does not reduce signal clarity.
- No asset resembles a protected game’s distinctive silhouette, UI chrome, icon, or faction styling.

## Mobile checks

- Crop or view screenshots at realistic phone size before signing off.
- Check both portrait and any alternate viewport states.
- Verify touch targets and interactive controls remain comfortably reachable.
- Prefer silhouette/value clarity over small detail.

## Required evidence for a confident pass

- Default/normal state.
- Busy/stress state.
- Critical feedback state.
- Reduced-effects or accessibility-relevant state when applicable.
- Before/after comparison when reviewing a change.
